Governance Decision Framework

This RACI matrix identifies the recurring governance decisions required throughout NIKE's transformation and assigns decision rights to appropriate executive roles. The matrix focuses on decisions rather than operational tasks and supports consistent governance from Crawl through Walk and Run.

RACI Key: R = Responsible, A = Accountable, C = Consulted, I = Informed. Each decision has exactly one Accountable role.

Accountable Role Rationales

1. Budget Allocation — John Donahoe, President & CEO

The CEO is accountable because transformation funding requires enterprise-level trade-offs across consumer experience, technology, data/analytics, and other strategic investments. Nike's Consumer Direct Acceleration strategy involved investment across digital consumer experiences, data and analytics, and an end-to-end technology foundation.

2. Architecture Standards — Andrew Campion, COO

The COO is accountable because Nike assigned Campion responsibility for global technology and digital transformation during the transformation period. This makes the role appropriate for resolving architecture decisions that affect whether capabilities developed during Crawl and Walk can integrate with Nike's broader technology foundation.

3. Data Governance — Andrew Campion, COO

The COO is accountable because the role included leadership of Nike's global technology and digital transformation. As Nike expanded its use of data and analytics across consumer and retail capabilities, consistent decisions about data quality, access, and integration would require enterprise-level technology governance.

4. Initiative Prioritization — John Donahoe, President & CEO

The CEO is accountable because prioritization requires resolving trade-offs across technology, consumer experience, financial investment, and organizational capacity. An enterprise-level decision maker is needed when resources must be shifted between initiatives or when an initiative must move, pause, or receive additional investment.

5. Vendor & Partnership Decisions — Andrew Campion, COO

The COO is accountable because Campion's responsibilities included both global technology and digital transformation and procurement. This combination makes the role appropriate for governing major technology and partnership decisions that affect the broader transformation.

6. Consumer Experience Scale-Up — Heidi O'Neill, President, Consumer & Marketplace

O'Neill is accountable because her organization was responsible for Nike's consumer and marketplace activities, including Nike Direct. This makes the role directly connected to deciding whether consumer-facing capabilities tested in limited deployments are ready to expand more broadly.

7. Transformation Operating Model — John Donahoe, President & CEO

The CEO is accountable because operating-model changes affect responsibilities and coordination across multiple executive functions. This is particularly relevant to Nike's transformation because the company made leadership and operating-model changes intended to streamline and accelerate execution of its Consumer Direct strategy.
