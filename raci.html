Governance Decision Framework

This RACI matrix identifies the recurring governance decisions required throughout NIKE's transformation and assigns decision rights to appropriate executive roles. The matrix focuses on decisions rather than operational tasks and supports consistent governance from Crawl through Walk and Run.

RACI Key: R = Responsible, A = Accountable, C = Consulted, I = Informed, A/R = the single Accountable role who also does the work. Each decision has exactly one Accountable role.
Scope of roles: Role titles reflect NIKE's executive team from April 2020, when the CEO, COO, CFO, and President of Consumer & Marketplace were in the roles shown. This covers the later Walk and Run phases. Governance for the 2017 start of Crawl would sit under the prior leadership, and we have not mapped that period.

Accountable Role Rationales

1. Budget Allocation — John Donahoe, President & CEO

Transformation funding requires enterprise-level trade-offs across consumer experience, data and analytics, and technology investment, which are the areas Consumer Direct Acceleration spans. Sources: NIKE FY2020 and FY2022 Form 10-K.

2. Architecture Standards — Andrew Campion, COO

From April 2020, Campion's remit included global technology and digital transformation, so he is positioned to decide whether Crawl and Walk capabilities fit the end-to-end technology foundation. Sources: NIKE leadership announcement, April 2020 (reported by Just Style and Retail Dive); NIKE FY2022 Form 10-K.

3. Data Governance — Andrew Campion, COO

Data quality, access, and integration standards need one enterprise technology owner, and Campion holds that role as COO. O'Neill is Responsible because consumer data originates in the Nike Direct and consumer organization she leads. Sources: NIKE leadership announcement, April 2020; NIKE FY2022 Form 10-K.

4. Initiative Prioritization — John Donahoe, President & CEO

When resources are constrained, trade-offs cross technology, consumer, finance, and organizational capacity, so the decision must sit above any single function. Source: team analysis based on the executive structure in the NIKE FY2022 Form 10-K.

5. Vendor & Partnership Decisions — Andrew Campion, COO

Campion's responsibilities included procurement alongside technology and digital transformation, which makes the COO the natural owner of major external technology relationships. Procurement later moved to the CFO in 2023, outside our window. Sources: NIKE leadership announcement, April 2020 (Just Style); NIKE press release, May 2023.

6. Consumer Experience Scale-Up — Heidi O'Neill, President, Consumer & Marketplace

O'Neill previously led Nike Direct and, from April 2020, led Nike's global sales and direct operations, so she owns the call on whether a Walk capability is ready for broader NIKE Direct or marketplace rollout. Sources: NIKE leadership announcement, April 2020 (Retail Dive); NIKE FY2022 Form 10-K.

7. Transformation Operating Model — John Donahoe, President & CEO

Changing responsibilities across several executive functions requires CEO authority. Donahoe's first major move in 2020 was reorganizing the senior team. Sources: Bloomberg, April 2020; NIKE FY2022 Form 10-K.

Concentration of authority: Campion is Accountable for three decisions (architecture, data governance, vendors) because his role combined technology and procurement. If these decisions came into conflict, the CEO would be the tiebreaker.
